### 1. Explanations

#### Unit 1: Where Meiosis Occurs

- **Meiosis** takes place specifically in **reproductive (sexual) cells**.
- These cells are located inside the **male and female sex organs** of most animals and plants.
- The main purpose of these reproductive cells is to produce specialized sex cells called **gametes**.

#### Unit 2: What Meiosis Is and What It Produces

- **Meiosis** is defined as the **nuclear division** of a parent reproductive cell.
- During this process, **one parent reproductive cell** divides to create **four daughter cells**.
- These four resulting daughter cells are called **gametes**.

#### Unit 3: Chromosome-Number Reduction

- Each **gamete** receives exactly **half the original chromosome number** found in the parent cell.
- In humans, a standard parent cell contains **46 chromosomes**.
- After meiosis, each human **gamete** contains **23 chromosomes**.

#### Unit 4: Quantitative Progression of Cell Division

- The step-by-step numerical progression of meiosis occurs in a clear sequence:
  1. **Starting cell:** **1 parent cell** containing **46 chromosomes**.
  2. **Intermediate stage:** The cell divides into **2 cells**, each containing **23 chromosomes**.
  3. **Final result:** Division finishes with **4 gametes**, each containing **23 chromosomes**.

#### Unit 5: Maintaining Chromosome Stability

- Halving the chromosome count during gamete formation is essential for reproduction.
- **Meiosis** plays a key role in maintaining **chromosome-number stability** across generations within a species.

### 2. Key Vocabulary

- **Meiosis:** The nuclear division of a parent reproductive cell that produces four daughter cells called gametes.
- **Reproductive (Sexual) Cells:** Specialized cells in the male and female sex organs of plants and animals that undergo meiosis.
- **Gametes:** The four daughter cells created by meiosis, each holding half the chromosome number of the parent cell.
- **Chromosome Reduction:** The halving of chromosome numbers from the parent cell (46 in humans) to the gametes (23 in humans).
- **Chromosome-Number Stability:** The process of keeping the number of chromosomes constant from one generation to the next in a species.
