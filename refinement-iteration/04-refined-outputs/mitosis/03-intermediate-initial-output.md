## 1. Explanations

### Purpose of Mitosis and Organism Types

- **Definition and Core Objective**: Mitosis (الانقسام المتساوي) is a process of nuclear cell division that produces two identical daughter cells from a single parent cell.
- **Reproduction in Unicellular Organisms**: In single-celled organisms such as *Paramecium* (البراميسيوم), mitosis functions directly as a mechanism of asexual reproduction to produce new individuals.
- **Growth and Repair in Humans**: In multicellular organisms such as humans, mitosis is essential for body growth and for replacing damaged or worn-out cells.
- **Chromosome Constancy**: Whether serving reproduction or tissue growth, mitosis maintains genetic continuity by ensuring that daughter cells receive the exact same chromosome number as the parent cell (such as 46 chromosomes in human body cells).

### Cell Structures Related to Mitosis

- **Chromatin Network (شبكة كروماتينية)**: Before cell division begins, genetic material inside the nucleus exists as long, thin, tangled threads known as the chromatin network.
- **Duplicated Chromosomes and Sister Chromatids (كروماتيدات شقيقة)**: Prior to division, DNA is duplicated, resulting in chromosomes made of two identical strands called sister chromatids.
- **Centromere (سنترومير)**: Sister chromatids are joined together at a central connection point called the centromere, which also serves as the attachment site for spindle fibers during division.
- **Spindle Fibers (خيوط المغزل) and Centrioles (مريكزات)**: Spindle fibers are fine structural threads that associate with the centromere of each chromosome to facilitate chromatid movement, while centrioles are specialized structures present in animal cells associated with the division process.

### Stages of Mitosis in Sequence

Mitosis progresses through a strict, continuous sequence of stages that links genetic duplication to physical cell separation:

1. **Interphase / Preparatory Phase (الطور البيني / التحضيري)**
   - This phase precedes active nuclear division; during this stage, **DNA is duplicated** so that an exact copy of the genetic material is prepared for the upcoming daughter cells.

2. **Prophase (الدور التمهيدي)**
   - As the first official stage of mitosis, chromosomes become visible as short, thick, duplicated structures.
   - Concurrently, both the **nuclear envelope** (الغلاف النووي) and the **nucleolus** (النوية) disappear to clear the central region of the cell.

3. **Metaphase (الدور الاستوائي)**
   - Duplicated chromosomes are arranged in the **middle of the cell**.
   - **Spindle fibers** become physically associated with the **centromere** of each chromosome.

4. **Anaphase (الدور الانفصالي)**
   - **Sister chromatids separate** from one another and move toward opposite poles of the cell, dividing the duplicated genetic material equally.

5. **Telophase (الدور النهائي)**
   - Chromosomes complete their movement and reach the opposite poles of the cell.
   - A new **nuclear envelope** re-forms around the chromosomes at each pole, organizing two new nuclei.

6. **Cytokinesis (انقسام السيتوبلازم)**
   - Cytokinesis follows nuclear division to complete the division of the cell cytoplasm into two distinct cells.
   - **Structural Differences by Cell Type**:
     - In **animal cells**, cytokinesis occurs through **membrane pinching / constriction** (تخصر الغشاء) from the outside inward.
     - In **plant cells**, cytokinesis occurs through the formation of a **cell plate / middle lamella** (تكون الصفيحة الوسطى) across the cell interior.

### Final Result of Mitosis

- **Cell Count and Genetic Identity**: Mitosis yields **two identical daughter cells** that are genetically identical to each other and to the parent cell.
- **Chromosome Number in Humans**: Human body cells contain **46 chromosomes**; following mitosis, each resulting daughter cell retains exactly **46 chromosomes**.

## 2. Key Vocabulary

1. **Mitosis (الانقسام المتساوي)**: A process of cell division in body cells that produces two identical daughter cells containing the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell.
2. **Interphase / Preparatory Phase (الطور البيني / التحضيري)**: The preparatory stage preceding mitosis during which DNA duplication takes place.
3. **Chromatin Network (شبكة كروماتينية)**: The unwound, tangled threads of genetic material present inside the nucleus before chromosomes become visible.
4. **Sister Chromatids (كروماتيدات شقيقة)**: Two identical copies of a duplicated chromosome connected together at the centromere.
5. **Centromere (سنترومير)**: The specific region holding sister chromatids together and associating with spindle fibers during cell division.
6. **Spindle Fibers (خيوط المغزل)**: Fine fiber structures that attach to centromeres and facilitate the movement of chromatids to opposite cell poles.
7. **Centrioles (مريكزات)**: Cell structures found in animal cells that are associated with the process of cell division.
8. **Prophase (الدور التمهيدي)**: The first stage of mitosis in which chromosomes become visible and the nuclear envelope and nucleolus disappear.
9. **Metaphase (الدور الاستوائي)**: The stage of mitosis in which duplicated chromosomes align in the middle of the cell.
10. **Anaphase (الدور الانفصالي)**: The stage of mitosis in which sister chromatids separate and move toward opposite poles.
11. **Telophase (الدور النهائي)**: The stage of mitosis in which chromosomes reach the poles and nuclear envelopes re-form around them.
12. **Cytokinesis (انقسام السيتوبلازم)**: The process of cytoplasmic division that completes cell separation following nuclear division.

## 4. Self-Assessment Questions

1. **Relating Duplication to Result**
   - **Question**: Why must DNA duplication occur during Interphase before active nuclear division begins?
   - *Answer*: DNA duplication ensures that genetic material is copied beforehand so that when the parent cell divides, each of the two daughter cells receives the exact same number of chromosomes as the parent cell.

2. **Connecting Structures to Stage Function**
   - **Question**: Describe the relationship between centromeres and spindle fibers during Metaphase and Anaphase.
   - *Answer*: During Metaphase, spindle fibers associate with the centromere of each chromosome arranged in the middle of the cell. During Anaphase, sister chromatids separate at the centromere and move toward opposite poles.

3. **Comparing Cytokinesis Mechanisms**
   - **Question**: How do animal cells and plant cells accomplish cytokinesis differently to complete cell division?
   - *Answer*: Animal cells complete division through membrane pinching / constriction, whereas plant cells complete division by constructing a cell plate / middle lamella.

4. **Applying Purpose Across Species**
   - **Question**: Compare the purpose and outcome of mitosis in a single-celled *Paramecium* to that in a human bone cell.
   - *Answer*: In *Paramecium*, mitosis serves the purpose of reproduction, whereas in a human bone cell, it serves growth and cell replacement. In both cases, the outcome is two identical daughter cells with the exact chromosome number of the original cell.
