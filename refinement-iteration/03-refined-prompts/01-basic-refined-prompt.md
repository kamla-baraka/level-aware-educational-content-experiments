# Meiosis – Basic Refined Prompt

## Experiment Condition

**Lesson:** Meiosis  
**Learner Level:** Basic  
**Iteration:** Pedagogical Profile Refinement

---

## Learning Objectives

The generated educational package must enable the learner to:

1. Identify the location of meiosis in reproductive/sexual cells in the male and female sex organs of plants and animals.
2. Define meiosis as nuclear division in a parent reproductive cell that produces four daughter cells called gametes.
3. Demonstrate chromosome-number reduction during meiosis, where each gamete contains half the chromosome number of the original cell, using the human example of 46 → 23 chromosomes.
4. Outline the quantitative result of meiosis: one parent reproductive cell produces four gametes, each containing half the original chromosome number.
5. Explain the biological significance of meiosis in contributing to the stability of chromosome number across generations.

---

## Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge

Use only the following curriculum knowledge.

### Learning Objective 1 – Location of Meiosis

- Meiosis occurs in reproductive/sexual cells responsible for producing gametes.
- These cells are located in the male and female sex organs of most animals and plants.

### Learning Objective 2 – Definition and Result

- Meiosis is a nuclear division of a parent reproductive cell.
- It produces four daughter cells called gametes.

### Learning Objective 3 – Chromosome-Number Reduction

- Each gamete contains half the chromosome number of the original parent cell.
- In humans, the parent cell has 46 chromosomes.
- The resulting gametes have 23 chromosomes each.

### Learning Objective 4 – Quantitative Progression

- One parent reproductive cell produces exactly four gametes.
- The curriculum representation shows:
  - one cell with 46 chromosomes;
  - then two cells with 23 chromosomes each;
  - then four gametes with 23 chromosomes each.

### Learning Objective 5 – Biological Significance

- Meiosis contributes directly to maintaining the stability of chromosome number in the species across generations.

## Basic Pedagogical Profile

Follow all of the following operational rules.

### Vocabulary Complexity

- Use simple and familiar Grade 8 language.
- Use required scientific terms from the curriculum.
- Explain each essential scientific term in simple words when it is first introduced.
- Avoid unnecessary academic wording.

### Sentence Complexity

- Use short and direct sentences.
- Each sentence should communicate one main idea whenever possible.

### Explanation Depth

- Explain one concept or one direct relationship at a time.
- Focus on the essential meaning.
- Do not require the learner to independently connect several relationships.

### Concept Load

- Present concepts in small, manageable units.
- Present related information sequentially rather than combining several concepts at once.

### Concept Relationships

- Present only simple and explicit relationships.
- Explain the relationship directly rather than requiring the learner to infer it.

### Scaffolding

- Provide high scaffolding.
- Use explicit instructions, cues, ordered steps, and guided support.
- Do not require the learner to infer important intermediate steps.

### Learner Independence

- Require low learner independence.
- Provide enough information and guidance for the learner to complete activities directly from the explanation.

### Cognitive Demand

Focus primarily on **Remember and Understand**.

Appropriate operations include:

- identify;
- define;
- recognize;
- list;
- order;
- describe; and
- directly explain information presented in the fixed curriculum knowledge.

Do not require:

- multi-concept analysis;
- independent justification;
- synthesis;
- extended inference; or
- integration of several relationships.

### Exercise Design

Use guided activities such as:

- matching;
- fill-in-the-blank;
- true/false;
- ordering;
- identification; and
- short direct-answer questions.

Avoid multi-step analytical tasks and independent justification.

### Self-Assessment Design

- Assess recall and direct understanding.
- Each question should normally contain one clear requirement.
- Questions should be answerable directly from the provided curriculum knowledge and explanations.

## Curriculum-Grounding Constraints

Use only the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge provided above.

Do not:

- introduce scientific facts outside the fixed knowledge;
- discuss detailed stages of meiosis;
- introduce Meiosis I or Meiosis II as additional scientific content;
- introduce crossing over or genetic variation;
- introduce Down syndrome or meiotic errors;
- introduce mitosis;
- add unsupported mechanisms or causal explanations;
- create new scientific examples;
- create hypothetical scientific scenarios;
- create new chromosome-number examples; or
- introduce higher-grade scientific knowledge.

Simplification must not change or expand the scientific content.
