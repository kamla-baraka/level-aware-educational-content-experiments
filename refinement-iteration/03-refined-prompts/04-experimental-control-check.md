# Experimental Control Check

## Purpose

Before generating the refined educational packages, the three level-specific prompts were reviewed to confirm that the experimental conditions remain controlled.

The purpose of this check is to ensure that differences in the generated outputs can be examined primarily in relation to the refined Pedagogical Profiles rather than changes in curriculum content, learning objectives, or output structure.

## Controlled Variables

The following elements are held constant across the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced conditions:

- Selected lesson: Meiosis.
- Grade level: Grade 8.
- Learning Objectives: the same five predefined objectives.
- Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge: identical curriculum knowledge boundary.
- Scientific content and core concepts.
- Curriculum-grounding requirement.
- Output structure:
  1. Explanations
  2. Key Vocabulary
  3. Exercises
  4. Self-Assessment Questions
- Restriction against external or higher-grade scientific knowledge.
- Restriction against unsupported mechanisms, causes, examples, and hypothetical scientific scenarios.

## Intended Independent Variable

The intended variable across the three generation conditions is the Pedagogical Profile.

The refined profiles operationalize level differentiation through:

- vocabulary treatment;
- sentence complexity;
- explanation depth;
- concept load;
- treatment of conceptual relationships;
- scaffolding;
- learner independence;
- cognitive demand;
- exercise design; and
- self-assessment design.

## Intended Level Progression

### Basic

The Basic condition emphasizes:

- simple and explicitly explained curriculum terminology;
- short and direct explanations;
- one concept or direct relationship at a time;
- high scaffolding;
- low learner independence;
- Remember and Understand cognitive operations; and
- guided exercises and direct self-assessment.

### Intermediate

The Intermediate condition emphasizes:

- standard curriculum terminology;
- explicit connections between two related pieces of curriculum information;
- moderate scaffolding;
- moderate learner independence;
- Understand and Apply cognitive operations; and
- comparison, sequencing, relationships, and simple application involving limited linked reasoning.

### Advanced

The Advanced condition emphasizes:

- accurate and independent use of curriculum terminology;
- integration of multiple pieces of fixed curriculum information;
- low scaffolding;
- high learner independence;
- Apply and Analyze cognitive operations; and
- multi-step interpretation, integration, conclusion, and evidence-based justification.

## Grounding Safeguards

Although the pedagogical treatment changes across learner levels, the scientific knowledge boundary does not change.

Higher-level difficulty must therefore be created through deeper processing of the same curriculum knowledge rather than through:

- additional scientific facts;
- higher-grade content;
- unsupported mechanisms or causal explanations;
- new scientific examples;
- hypothetical scientific scenarios;
- unsupported numerical cases; or
- increased text length alone.

## Control Check Result

The refined prompts preserve the same lesson, Learning Objectives, Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge, scientific boundary, and output structure across the three learner levels.

The intended experimental variation is therefore concentrated in the operational Pedagogical Profile applied to the same curriculum knowledge.

This controlled setup will be used to examine whether the refined profiles produce clearer observable differentiation than the initial experiment, particularly in the previously weaker criteria of vocabulary complexity, explanation depth, and scaffolding.

The results will be interpreted using the same predefined comparison criteria used in the initial experiments.
