# Refined Pedagogical Profiles

## Purpose

The refined Pedagogical Profiles translate the intended Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced learner levels into explicit and observable generation rules.

The refinement is based on the findings of the initial Mitosis and Meiosis experiments, which showed that differentiation was clearer in cognitive demand, exercise difficulty, and self-assessment difficulty, but weaker in vocabulary complexity, explanation depth, and scaffolding.

The refined profiles therefore aim to strengthen the operational distinction among learner levels without changing the curriculum knowledge boundary or the scientific Learning Objectives.

---

## Common Constraints Across All Levels

The following requirements remain fixed across Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced:

- Use only the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge.
- Maintain the same scientific Learning Objectives.
- Do not introduce external or higher-grade scientific knowledge.
- Do not introduce unsupported scientific mechanisms, causes, examples, or hypothetical scientific facts.
- Maintain scientific accuracy and the same scientific core across all levels.
- Maintain the same four-component Output Structure:
  1. Explanations
  2. Key Vocabulary
  3. Exercises
  4. Self-Assessment Questions
- Level differentiation must result from pedagogical treatment rather than from adding different scientific knowledge.
- Increased text length alone must not be used as a method of creating a higher learner level.

# Basic Pedagogical Profile

| Criterion | Operational Rule |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary Complexity** | Use simple, familiar Grade 8 language. Required scientific terms from the curriculum must still be used, but each essential scientific term should be explained in simple words when first introduced. Avoid unnecessary academic wording. |
| **Sentence Complexity** | Use short and direct sentences. Each sentence should communicate one main idea whenever possible. |
| **Explanation Depth** | Explain one concept or one direct relationship at a time. Focus on essential meaning and avoid requiring the learner to connect several relationships independently. |
| **Concept Load** | Present concepts in small, manageable units. Avoid introducing several related concepts simultaneously when they can be presented sequentially. |
| **Concept Relationships** | Present only simple and explicit relationships between concepts. The relationship should be directly explained rather than left for the learner to infer. |
| **Scaffolding Level** | Provide high scaffolding through explicit instructions, cues, ordered steps, and guided support. Do not require the learner to infer important intermediate steps. |
| **Learner Independence** | Require low learner independence. Provide sufficient information and guidance for the learner to complete activities directly from the provided explanation. |
| **Cognitive Demand** | Focus primarily on Remember and Understand. Appropriate operations include identify, define, recognize, list, order, describe, and directly explain information presented in the curriculum knowledge. |
| **Exercise Design** | Use guided activities such as matching, fill-in-the-blank, true/false, ordering, identification, and short direct-answer questions. Avoid multi-step analysis, independent justification, and integration of several concepts. |
| **Self-Assessment Design** | Assess recall and direct understanding of essential curriculum knowledge. Each question should normally contain one clear requirement and should not depend on multi-step reasoning. |
| **Grounding Constraint** | Simplification must remain within the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge and must not introduce new scientific facts, examples, mechanisms, causes, or explanations. |

# Intermediate Pedagogical Profile

| Criterion | Operational Rule |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary Complexity** | Use standard Grade 8 curriculum terminology directly. Briefly clarify a scientific term only when needed for understanding. Use moderately developed academic language, but do not introduce scientific terminology outside the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. |
| **Sentence Complexity** | Use moderately developed sentences that may connect two related ideas. Avoid both overly simplified sentence patterns and unnecessarily dense academic phrasing. |
| **Explanation Depth** | Explain concepts and explicitly connect two related concepts or ideas when the relationship is supported by the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. Move beyond isolated definitions by showing how related information connects, without requiring integration of several concepts at once. |
| **Concept Load** | Present several related concepts within the same section when appropriate, but organize them clearly. The learner may be required to work with two related concepts or pieces of information at a time. |
| **Concept Relationships** | Require the learner to recognize, explain, compare, or apply a relationship between two explicitly presented concepts or pieces of curriculum information. Do not require independent integration of multiple relationships simultaneously. |
| **Scaffolding Level** | Provide moderate scaffolding. Give the task goal and essential information, but leave part of the reasoning, comparison, sequencing, or application for the learner to complete. Avoid providing every intermediate reasoning step. |
| **Learner Independence** | Require moderate learner independence. The learner should use the provided curriculum information to complete a relationship, comparison, sequence, or simple application without being given the complete reasoning process. |
| **Cognitive Demand** | Focus primarily on Understand and Apply. Appropriate operations include explain, compare, classify, sequence, relate, and apply information to a simple situation or data already contained within the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. |
| **Exercise Design** | Use tasks such as guided comparison, sequencing with explanation, completing relationships, interpreting simple curriculum-provided information, and simple application questions. Tasks may require two linked reasoning steps but should not require independent integration of multiple concepts or extended justification. |
| **Self-Assessment Design** | Assess understanding and simple application. Questions may require the learner to explain a relationship, compare two curriculum concepts, interpret a simple sequence or numerical relationship, or apply explicitly provided knowledge. |
| **Grounding Constraint** | All relationships, comparisons, applications, and examples must be derived from the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. Do not create new scientific scenarios, mechanisms, causal explanations, numerical examples, or facts in order to increase difficulty. |

# Advanced Pedagogical Profile

| Criterion | Operational Rule |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary Complexity** | Use full Grade 8 curriculum terminology accurately and independently, without repeatedly simplifying terms that have already been established. Use academically developed language to express relationships among curriculum concepts, but do not introduce scientific terminology outside the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. |
| **Sentence Complexity** | Use developed but Grade-8-appropriate sentences that can express relationships among multiple ideas. Increased sentence complexity must serve conceptual integration rather than simply making the text longer or more difficult to read. |
| **Explanation Depth** | Integrate multiple related concepts from the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge within the same explanation. Emphasize how several pieces of curriculum information relate to one another, without adding mechanisms, causes, or scientific explanations not present in the fixed knowledge. |
| **Concept Load** | Allow the learner to work with three or more related pieces of fixed curriculum information when the lesson content supports this. Concepts should be integrated rather than merely listed together. |
| **Concept Relationships** | Require the learner to connect, interpret, or integrate multiple explicitly supported relationships. The learner may be asked to use several pieces of fixed curriculum evidence to reach a conclusion or justify an answer. |
| **Scaffolding Level** | Provide low scaffolding. State the task and provide only the information necessary to define its scope. Do not provide the sequence of reasoning steps when the learner can derive them from the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. |
| **Learner Independence** | Require high learner independence within the curriculum boundary. The learner should independently organize, connect, interpret, and justify using the provided curriculum knowledge. |
| **Cognitive Demand** | Focus primarily on Apply and Analyze. Appropriate operations include analyze, interpret, integrate, distinguish, conclude, justify, and explain relationships using evidence contained in the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. |
| **Exercise Design** | Use multi-step tasks that require integration of multiple fixed curriculum concepts, interpretation of curriculum-provided information, evidence-based justification, relationship mapping, or conclusions derived from several pieces of provided knowledge. Do not increase difficulty by introducing external scientific scenarios or information. |
| **Self-Assessment Design** | Require independent interpretation, integration, or justification using multiple pieces of fixed curriculum knowledge. Questions may ask the learner to support an answer with curriculum evidence or explain how several provided concepts are connected. |
| **Grounding Constraint** | All analysis, integration, conclusions, and justification must remain derivable from the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge. Do not introduce external mechanisms, hypothetical scientific conditions, unsupported causes, higher-grade content, or new factual information to create advanced difficulty. |

# Operational Differentiation Summary

The following summary defines the intended observable distinction among the three refined Pedagogical Profiles.

| Dimension | Basic | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary Use** | Required scientific terms are introduced with immediate simple explanation. | Standard curriculum terminology is used directly, with clarification only when needed. | Full curriculum terminology is used independently and relationally without repeated simplification. |
| **Sentence Complexity** | Short, direct sentences; mainly one idea per sentence. | Moderately developed sentences connecting up to two related ideas. | Developed Grade-8-appropriate sentences expressing relationships among multiple ideas. |
| **Explanation Depth** | One concept or one direct relationship at a time. | Explicit connection between two related concepts or pieces of information. | Integration of multiple related concepts and relationships from the fixed knowledge. |
| **Concept Load** | Small, sequential units. | Usually two related concepts or pieces of information are processed together. | Three or more related pieces of information may be integrated when supported by the lesson. |
| **Concept Relationships** | Relationships are explicitly explained to the learner. | Learner processes, compares, or applies one supported relationship. | Learner independently connects or integrates multiple supported relationships. |
| **Scaffolding** | High: explicit instructions, cues, ordered steps, and guided support. | Moderate: task and essential information are provided, but part of the reasoning is left to the learner. | Low: task and scope are provided, but reasoning organization is primarily left to the learner. |
| **Learner Independence** | Low | Moderate | High within the fixed curriculum boundary |
| **Primary Cognitive Demand** | Remember / Understand | Understand / Apply | Apply / Analyze |
| **Typical Operations** | Identify, define, recognize, list, order, describe | Explain, compare, classify, sequence, relate, simple application | Analyze, interpret, integrate, distinguish, conclude, justify |
| **Exercise Structure** | Guided, mainly single-step tasks. | Guided-to-partially-independent tasks, potentially involving two linked reasoning steps. | Multi-step tasks requiring integration or evidence-based reasoning. |
| **Self-Assessment** | Recall and direct understanding; normally one clear requirement per question. | Understanding and simple application of relationships. | Independent interpretation, integration, and justification using multiple pieces of fixed knowledge. |
| **Scientific Knowledge Boundary** | Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge only | Same Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge | Same Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge |

---

## Key Differentiation Rule

The learner levels must differ in **how the same curriculum knowledge is processed**, not in the amount of external scientific knowledge provided.

A higher learner level must therefore not be created merely by:

- increasing text length;
- using unnecessarily difficult wording;
- adding scientific facts outside the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge;
- adding unsupported mechanisms or causal explanations;
- introducing higher-grade scientific content; or
- creating hypothetical scientific scenarios that require knowledge outside the fixed curriculum boundary.

Instead, higher levels should be created through progressively greater:

- conceptual connection;
- learner independence;
- cognitive processing;
- application;
- integration; and
- evidence-based reasoning.

## Use in the Refinement Experiment

These refined profiles will be used as explicit generation constraints in the next controlled iteration.

The resulting Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced packages will be evaluated using the same predefined comparison criteria used in the initial experiments.

The purpose of the next iteration is to determine whether these more operational profile definitions produce clearer observable differentiation while maintaining curriculum grounding and scientific core consistency.
