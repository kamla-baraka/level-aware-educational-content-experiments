# Refined Cross-Experiment Comparison: Meiosis vs Mitosis

## Purpose

This analysis compares the refined Meiosis and refined Mitosis experiments using the same ten predefined criteria.

The purpose is to examine whether the same refined pedagogical profiles produced a similar pattern of level differentiation across two different lesson conditions while maintaining curriculum grounding, learning-objective coverage, and scientific-core consistency.

The refined pedagogical profiles were kept unchanged across the two lesson conditions. Therefore, the lesson content changed, while the intended Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced pedagogical characteristics remained fixed.

This comparison is based on the final accepted Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced outputs from both refined experiments.

---

## Comparison Basis

The same ten predefined criteria are used:

1. Curriculum Grounding
2. Learning Objectives Coverage
3. Vocabulary Complexity
4. Explanation Depth
5. Scaffolding Level
6. Cognitive Demand
7. Exercise Difficulty
8. Self-Assessment Difficulty
9. Scientific Core Consistency
10. Overall Level Differentiation

The criteria are divided into two groups.

### Control Criteria

These criteria are expected to remain stable across learner levels:

- Curriculum Grounding
- Learning Objectives Coverage
- Scientific Core Consistency

### Differentiation Criteria

These criteria are expected to vary according to the learner-level pedagogical profile:

- Vocabulary Complexity
- Explanation Depth
- Scaffolding Level
- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

Text length alone is not considered evidence of successful level differentiation.

# 1. Curriculum Grounding

## Refined Meiosis

The final Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced Meiosis outputs remained within the predefined curriculum knowledge after targeted grounding revisions.

The revisions primarily removed unsupported causal interpretations, hypothetical numerical extensions, and information beyond the selected curriculum scope.

## Refined Mitosis

The final Mitosis outputs also remained substantially within the fixed curriculum boundary after targeted revisions.

The Advanced initial generation required particular attention because increased analytical complexity encouraged unsupported hypothetical scenarios, causal deductions, and broader relationships.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**No differentiation expected – Control criterion**

Both lesson conditions demonstrated that level differentiation could be maintained without intentionally assigning additional scientific knowledge to higher learner levels.

However, both experiments also showed that grounding required active control during generation and revision.

---

# 2. Learning Objectives Coverage

## Refined Meiosis

All five fixed Meiosis learning objectives were represented across the three learner-level packages.

## Refined Mitosis

All five fixed Mitosis learning objectives were represented across the three learner-level packages.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**No differentiation expected – Control criterion**

The learner levels differed in how the objectives were processed rather than in which objectives were included.

This pattern was consistent across both lessons.

---

# 3. Vocabulary Complexity

## Refined Meiosis

**Moderate differentiation**

The same essential curriculum terminology was retained across levels.

The distinction appeared mainly in how terms were used within direct, relational, or integrated explanations and tasks.

## Refined Mitosis

**Moderate differentiation**

A similar pattern was observed.

Basic used curriculum terminology more directly, Intermediate connected terminology across related facts, and Advanced used the same terms within broader analytical relationships.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**Similar pattern across both lessons**

Vocabulary complexity was not the strongest dimension of differentiation in either experiment.

This appears consistent with the experimental decision to maintain the same Grade 8 curriculum terminology across learner levels rather than introducing additional higher-grade terminology.

---

# 4. Explanation Depth

## Refined Meiosis

**Clear differentiation**

Basic explanations were direct and segmented.

Intermediate explanations connected related curriculum facts.

Advanced explanations required broader integration of multiple fixed relationships.

## Refined Mitosis

**Clear differentiation**

The same progression was observed:

- Basic: direct and sequential presentation;
- Intermediate: explicit relationships between related facts;
- Advanced: integrated representation of multiple stages, structures, purposes, and outcomes.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**Consistent clear differentiation**

Explanation depth showed a similar Basic → Intermediate → Advanced progression in both lesson conditions.

This suggests that the refined profiles provided sufficiently operational guidance for controlling the depth and relational structure of explanations in these two controlled experiments.

---

# 5. Scaffolding Level

## Refined Meiosis

**Clear differentiation**

Basic provided high instructional support, Intermediate reduced that support while retaining guidance, and Advanced required substantially greater learner independence.

## Refined Mitosis

**Clear differentiation**

The same pattern appeared through:

- highly guided Basic tasks;
- guided but relational Intermediate tasks;
- lower-scaffolded Advanced tasks requiring independent integration and justification.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**Consistent clear differentiation**

Scaffolding was one of the strongest and most reproducible dimensions across the two refined lesson conditions.

The operational descriptions of high, moderate, and low scaffolding appeared to produce observable differences in task structure.

---

# 6. Cognitive Demand

## Refined Meiosis

**Clear differentiation**

The progression generally followed:

- Basic: Remember + Understand
- Intermediate: Understand + Apply
- Advanced: Apply + Analyze

## Refined Mitosis

**Clear differentiation**

The same intended progression was observable.

Advanced tasks required multi-step processing and analysis using several fixed curriculum facts, whereas Basic tasks focused more strongly on direct recognition and understanding.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**Consistent clear differentiation**

Cognitive demand remained a strong differentiating dimension across both lessons.

The higher learner level did not require additional scientific knowledge; instead, it required more complex processing of the same knowledge.

---

# 7. Exercise Difficulty

## Refined Meiosis

**Clear differentiation**

The progression moved from highly guided tasks to comparison and sequencing, and finally to multi-concept integration, evaluation, and justification.

## Refined Mitosis

**Clear differentiation**

A similar progression was observed:

- Basic: direct recognition, ordering, and identification;
- Intermediate: comparison, classification, correction, and simple application;
- Advanced: multi-fact evaluation, relationship mapping, multi-step integration, and justification.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**Consistent clear differentiation**

Exercise difficulty showed a stable pattern across both refined lesson conditions.

Importantly, difficulty was expressed primarily through cognitive processing and task structure rather than through additional curriculum content.

---

# 8. Self-Assessment Difficulty

## Refined Meiosis

**Clear differentiation**

Basic self-assessment focused on direct understanding, Intermediate required connected reasoning, and Advanced required integration and analysis.

## Refined Mitosis

**Clear differentiation**

The same progression was observed, culminating in Advanced questions that integrated several stages, structures, purposes, and quantitative outcomes.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**Consistent clear differentiation**

Self-assessment difficulty followed a similar level progression across both lesson conditions.

---

# 9. Scientific Core Consistency

## Refined Meiosis

The same fixed scientific content was retained across the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced conditions.

## Refined Mitosis

The same fixed scientific core was likewise retained across learner levels after grounding revision.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**No differentiation expected – Control criterion**

Scientific-core consistency remained stable in both experiments.

This supports the intended experimental distinction between:

- changing the pedagogical treatment of knowledge; and
- changing the scientific knowledge itself.

The experiments targeted the former rather than the latter.

# 10. Overall Level Differentiation

## Refined Meiosis

The refined Meiosis experiment showed clearer and more systematic differentiation than the original experiment.

The strongest distinctions occurred in:

- explanation depth;
- scaffolding;
- cognitive demand;
- exercise difficulty;
- self-assessment difficulty.

Vocabulary differentiation remained moderate.

## Refined Mitosis

The refined Mitosis experiment showed the same general pattern.

The strongest distinctions occurred in the same five dimensions, while vocabulary complexity remained more moderately differentiated.

## Cross-Experiment Observation

**A similar overall differentiation pattern was observed across both refined lesson conditions.**

The Basic condition was consistently characterized by:

- high scaffolding;
- direct presentation;
- limited conceptual load;
- direct understanding and recall;
- guided tasks.

The Intermediate condition was consistently characterized by:

- moderate scaffolding;
- explicit connections between related facts;
- guided comparison and sequencing;
- simple application;
- moderate learner independence.

The Advanced condition was consistently characterized by:

- lower scaffolding;
- integration of multiple fixed facts;
- multi-step reasoning;
- analysis;
- evaluation and correction;
- evidence-based justification;
- greater learner independence.

# Cross-Experiment Finding

Across the two refined lesson conditions, the same operationalized pedagogical profiles were associated with a similar pattern of generated level differentiation.

In both Meiosis and Mitosis:

- Vocabulary Complexity showed **Moderate differentiation**.
- Explanation Depth showed **Clear differentiation**.
- Scaffolding Level showed **Clear differentiation**.
- Cognitive Demand showed **Clear differentiation**.
- Exercise Difficulty showed **Clear differentiation**.
- Self-Assessment Difficulty showed **Clear differentiation**.

At the same time:

- Curriculum Grounding remained a control.
- Learning Objectives Coverage remained a control.
- Scientific Core Consistency remained a control.

This consistency across the two controlled lesson conditions provides preliminary evidence that the refined pedagogical profiles can produce observable level-aware differences without requiring different scientific knowledge for each learner level.

---

# Original-to-Refined Pattern Across Both Lessons

The original experiments showed partial and uneven differentiation.

In particular, the weaker dimensions were:

- vocabulary complexity;
- explanation depth;
- scaffolding.

After operationalizing the pedagogical profiles, both refined lesson conditions showed the following pattern:

| Differentiation Dimension | Original Pattern | Refined Pattern Across Both Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary Complexity | Limited | Moderate |
| Explanation Depth | Limited–Moderate / Moderate | Clear |
| Scaffolding Level | Moderate / Partial | Clear |
| Cognitive Demand | Clear | Clear |
| Exercise Difficulty | Clear | Clear |
| Self-Assessment Difficulty | Clear | Clear |

The most observable improvement therefore occurred in explanation depth and scaffolding, while vocabulary differentiation improved more moderately.

Cognitive demand, exercise difficulty, and self-assessment difficulty were already relatively strong in the original experiments and remained strong after refinement.

---

# Grounding Observation Across Experiments

The refinement experiments also revealed a repeated grounding issue.

When the Advanced condition required greater integration and analytical reasoning, the model sometimes attempted to extend beyond the predefined curriculum knowledge by introducing:

- additional causal interpretations;
- hypothetical scenarios;
- broader relationships;
- unsupported numerical or biological deductions.

Targeted grounding revisions were therefore required in both lesson conditions.

This repeated observation suggests that pedagogical complexity and grounding control should be treated as separate but interacting requirements in the proposed framework.

The pedagogical profile determines:

**HOW the retrieved knowledge should be processed and presented.**

The grounding mechanism determines:

**WHAT knowledge may be used.**

The current experiments provide preliminary design evidence for maintaining this distinction.

They do not establish a general causal relationship between cognitive complexity and grounding errors.

---

# Design Implication for the Proposed Framework

The cross-experiment results support retaining the refined operational pedagogical profiles as the working level definitions for the next framework-design stage.

The profiles should not operate independently.

They should be applied after curriculum retrieval and under explicit grounding constraints.

This is consistent with the proposed conceptual flow:

**Learner Assessment → Learner Level → Pedagogical Profile → Curriculum Retrieval → Level-Aware Generation → Assessment → Profile Update**

Within this flow:

- learner assessment identifies the learner level;
- the pedagogical profile specifies the intended instructional treatment;
- curriculum retrieval provides the approved knowledge;
- level-aware generation combines the retrieved knowledge with the pedagogical profile;
- grounding constraints limit generation to the approved knowledge boundary;
- assessment supports subsequent learner-level recommendations.

---

# Limitations

The cross-experiment analysis remains limited in several ways.

First, only two lessons from one selected Grade 8 science chapter were examined.

Second, the comparison is researcher-based rather than expert-validated.

Third, the experiments evaluate generated educational artifacts rather than actual learner outcomes.

Fourth, the experiments functionally controlled curriculum grounding but did not implement the complete technical RAG pipeline using independently configured chunking, embeddings, vector retrieval, and similarity search.

Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as controlled proof-of-concept evidence rather than validation of the complete framework.

---

# Conclusion

The refined Meiosis and Mitosis experiments showed a similar pattern of learner-level differentiation when the same operationalized pedagogical profiles were applied across two controlled lesson conditions.

The clearest and most consistent distinctions were observed in explanation depth, scaffolding, cognitive demand, exercise difficulty, and self-assessment difficulty.

Vocabulary complexity remained more moderately differentiated because the same curriculum terminology was intentionally preserved across levels.

The control criteria remained substantially stable, indicating that the observed differentiation was based primarily on pedagogical treatment and learner processing rather than on providing different scientific knowledge to different levels.

These findings provide preliminary proof-of-concept evidence supporting the use of operational pedagogical profiles in the proposed level-aware generation component.

They do not establish educational effectiveness, learner-performance improvement, expert validation, or generalizability beyond the current controlled experiments.
