# Refined Mitosis – Criterion-Based Comparison

## Purpose

This analysis evaluates the final Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced outputs generated for the refined Mitosis experiment.

The purpose is to determine whether the operationalized pedagogical profiles produced observable level differentiation while maintaining the same curriculum knowledge, learning objectives, scientific core, and output structure across the three conditions.

Only the final accepted outputs are used for level comparison. Initial outputs and revisions are retained separately as evidence of grounding and refinement behavior.

---

## Comparison Basis

The three final outputs were compared using the same ten predefined criteria used in the original experiments and in the refined Meiosis analysis:

1. Curriculum Grounding
2. Learning Objectives Coverage
3. Vocabulary Complexity
4. Explanation Depth
5. Scaffolding Level
6. Cognitive Demand
7. Exercise Difficulty
8. Self-Assessment Difficulty
9. Scientific Core Consistency
10. Overall Level Differentiation

The criteria are interpreted in two groups:

### Control Criteria

These criteria are expected to remain stable rather than differ by learner level:

- Curriculum Grounding
- Learning Objectives Coverage
- Scientific Core Consistency

### Differentiation Criteria

These criteria are expected to vary according to the pedagogical profile:

- Vocabulary Complexity
- Explanation Depth
- Scaffolding Level
- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

Text length alone is not treated as evidence of successful differentiation.

---

# Criterion-Based Analysis

## 1. Curriculum Grounding

### Basic

The final Basic output remained closely aligned with the fixed curriculum knowledge. It covered the purpose of mitosis, the fixed sequence of stages, major stage events, relevant structures, cytokinesis, and the final chromosome-number outcome without intentionally introducing additional scientific mechanisms.

### Intermediate

The final Intermediate output remained within the same fixed curriculum boundary after targeted grounding revision. Unsupported causal explanations and external applications identified in the initial generation were removed.

### Advanced

The final Advanced output was substantially improved after targeted grounding revision. The hypothetical laboratory disruption scenarios and several unsupported causal interpretations from the initial generation were removed.

A minor researcher observation remained in one comparative matrix, where some general mitosis outcomes were distributed across organism/cell-type categories more broadly than the explicitly assigned fixed relationships. This was documented transparently and was not treated as evidence of level differentiation.

### Classification

**No differentiation expected – Control criterion**

### Interpretation

Curriculum grounding was maintained across the three final conditions. Differences in learner level were therefore not intentionally created by adding scientific knowledge.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives Coverage

### Basic

The Basic package addressed all five fixed learning objectives through direct explanations, vocabulary, guided exercises, and direct self-assessment questions.

### Intermediate

The Intermediate package addressed the same five objectives while requiring learners to connect related facts, compare curriculum relationships, sequence events, and apply fixed information.

### Advanced

The Advanced package addressed the same objectives through multi-stage integration, relationship analysis, multi-fact evaluation, and quantitative and structural application.

### Classification

**No differentiation expected – Control criterion**

### Interpretation

All three conditions maintained the same learning-objective coverage. The intended distinction concerned how learners processed the objectives rather than which objectives were taught.

---

## 3. Vocabulary Complexity

### Basic Evidence

The Basic output used the essential curriculum terminology and presented terms in relatively direct and accessible formulations.

Vocabulary included terms such as:

- Mitosis
- Interphase
- Prophase
- Metaphase
- Anaphase
- Telophase
- Cytokinesis
- Chromatin
- Sister chromatids
- Centromere
- Spindle fibers
- Centrioles

Definitions were generally short and directly connected to the curriculum fact represented by each term.

### Intermediate Evidence

The Intermediate output retained the same curriculum terminology but used it within more relational explanations and tasks.

The learner was expected to connect terms such as:

- spindle fibers and centromere;
- Metaphase and Anaphase;
- animal and plant cytokinesis;
- chromosome number and final mitosis outcome.

### Advanced Evidence

The Advanced output continued to use the same scientific vocabulary because all three levels were based on the same Grade 8 curriculum content.

However, terminology was used within more integrated expressions such as:

- multi-stage structural relationships;
- stage–structure–outcome connections;
- chromosomal and cellular output;
- structural components involved in mitosis.

### Classification

**Moderate differentiation**

### Interpretation

Vocabulary differentiation was observable but intentionally limited. The Advanced condition did not introduce higher-grade scientific terminology merely to appear more difficult.

The main difference was therefore in how curriculum terminology was used and connected rather than in introducing substantially different vocabulary sets.

---

## 4. Explanation Depth

### Basic Evidence

The Basic explanations presented information in small, sequential units.

For example, stage descriptions generally focused on one principal event at a time:

- DNA is duplicated during Interphase.
- Chromosomes become visible during Prophase.
- Chromosomes are arranged in the middle during Metaphase.
- Sister chromatids separate during Anaphase.
- Nuclear structures re-form during Telophase.

This reflects high instructional support and limited conceptual load.

### Intermediate Evidence

The Intermediate explanations connected related curriculum facts more explicitly.

For example:

- chromosome arrangement in Metaphase was connected with spindle-fiber association at the centromere;
- the sequence of stages was presented together with their related structural changes;
- purpose, final result, and chromosome number were explained using moderately connected statements.

The learner therefore encountered relationships between related concepts rather than isolated facts alone.

### Advanced Evidence

The Advanced explanations organized multiple curriculum relationships into broader conceptual groupings:

- purpose and biological context;
- structural components involved in mitosis;
- sequential dynamics of division;
- quantitative outcome.

The output required the learner to understand how several fixed facts fit into a larger representation of the process without introducing additional scientific knowledge.

### Classification

**Clear differentiation**

### Interpretation

Explanation depth progressed from direct and sequential presentation in Basic, to connected relationships in Intermediate, to broader integration of multiple curriculum concepts in Advanced.

The distinction was based on conceptual organization and relational depth rather than text length alone.

---

## 5. Scaffolding Level

### Basic Evidence

The Basic condition provided high scaffolding.

Tasks relied primarily on:

- direct identification;
- matching;
- simple ordering;
- fill-in or short-answer formats;
- direct retrieval of curriculum facts.

The learner was given substantial structural guidance.

### Intermediate Evidence

The Intermediate condition reduced scaffolding by requiring:

- guided comparisons;
- classification;
- correction of statements;
- sequencing with explanation;
- linking two related curriculum facts;
- simple application.

The task structure still guided the learner, but answers required more independent processing.

### Advanced Evidence

The Advanced condition used substantially lower scaffolding.

Learners were asked to:

- evaluate multi-part statements;
- identify multiple errors;
- provide corrections and justification;
- map relationships across several stages;
- integrate multiple fixed curriculum facts;
- construct unified explanations from several pieces of evidence.

### Classification

**Clear differentiation**

### Interpretation

A clear progression in learner support was observed:

**Basic: high scaffolding → Intermediate: moderate scaffolding → Advanced: low scaffolding**

This represents an observable pedagogical distinction rather than a difference in content quantity alone.

---

## 6. Cognitive Demand

### Basic Evidence

The Basic condition primarily targeted **Remember and Understand**.

Learners were generally asked to:

- identify;
- recall;
- recognize;
- order;
- state direct facts.

### Intermediate Evidence

The Intermediate condition moved toward **Understand and Apply**.

Learners were asked to:

- compare;
- classify;
- connect;
- correct;
- sequence with explanation;
- apply fixed information to straightforward tasks.

### Advanced Evidence

The Advanced condition primarily targeted **Apply and Analyze**.

Learners were required to:

- evaluate multi-part statements;
- detect multiple errors;
- justify corrections;
- integrate three or more curriculum facts;
- analyze stage–structure relationships;
- connect purpose, chromosome number, stages, structures, and final outcomes.

### Classification

**Clear differentiation**

### Interpretation

Cognitive demand showed a systematic progression consistent with the refined pedagogical profiles.

The Advanced condition required deeper processing of the same curriculum knowledge rather than additional scientific knowledge.

---

## 7. Exercise Difficulty

### Basic Evidence

Basic exercises were highly guided and focused on direct curriculum recognition and understanding.

Typical demands included:

- matching stages with events;
- ordering stages;
- identifying structures;
- completing direct statements.

### Intermediate Evidence

Intermediate exercises required more relational processing.

Typical demands included:

- guided comparison;
- statement correction;
- classification;
- sequencing combined with explanation;
- connecting two related curriculum facts;
- simple application using the fixed human chromosome number.

### Advanced Evidence

Advanced exercises required substantially greater integration and independence.

Examples included:

- evaluating multi-part statements containing more than one claim;
- identifying and correcting multiple errors;
- integrating Interphase, Metaphase, and Anaphase in one analytical task;
- integrating Prophase, Telophase, and Cytokinesis;
- tracing structural and quantitative information across multiple steps.

### Classification

**Clear differentiation**

### Interpretation

Exercise difficulty increased through the type and number of relationships the learner had to process, not merely through longer questions.

---

## 8. Self-Assessment Difficulty

### Basic Evidence

Basic self-assessment primarily checked direct recall and understanding of:

- stage sequence;
- stage events;
- structures;
- purpose;
- final mitosis outcome.

### Intermediate Evidence

Intermediate self-assessment required learners to connect related concepts, such as:

- DNA duplication and final chromosome number;
- spindle fibers, centromere, Metaphase, and Anaphase;
- animal versus plant cytokinesis;
- purpose of mitosis across curriculum examples.

### Advanced Evidence

Advanced self-assessment required broader integration across multiple fixed facts.

Examples included:

- tracing structural events across several stages;
- analyzing relationships among centromere, spindle fibers, chromosome arrangement, and chromatid movement;
- comparing cytokinesis while relating it to completion of cell division;
- integrating purpose in Paramecium, purpose in humans, human chromosome number, and final mitosis outcome into a unified response.

### Classification

**Clear differentiation**

### Interpretation

Self-assessment difficulty progressed from direct understanding to relational application and finally to multi-concept analytical integration.

---

## 9. Scientific Core Consistency

### Basic

The Basic package represented the fixed scientific core of the Mitosis lesson.

### Intermediate

The Intermediate package retained the same scientific core while changing the expected level of processing.

### Advanced

The Advanced package retained the same scientific core after grounding revision while increasing analytical integration and learner independence.

### Classification

**No differentiation expected – Control criterion**

### Interpretation

The scientific content itself remained substantially stable across the three conditions.

This is important because the intended learner-level distinction was produced through pedagogical treatment rather than by assigning different scientific content to each level.

---

## 10. Overall Level Differentiation

### Observed Pattern

The refined Mitosis outputs showed an observable progression across the three conditions.

**Basic** was characterized by:

- short and direct presentation;
- high scaffolding;
- limited conceptual load;
- direct recall and understanding;
- guided exercises and self-assessment.

**Intermediate** was characterized by:

- explicit connections between related curriculum facts;
- moderate scaffolding;
- guided comparison and classification;
- sequencing with explanation;
- simple application;
- moderate learner independence.

**Advanced** was characterized by:

- integration of multiple fixed curriculum facts;
- multi-stage relationship analysis;
- multi-step reasoning;
- evaluation and correction of complex statements;
- evidence-based justification;
- lower scaffolding;
- greater learner independence.

### Classification

**Clear overall differentiation, with vocabulary differentiation remaining more moderate than the other pedagogical dimensions.**

# Overall Finding

The refined Mitosis iteration showed clearer and more systematic differentiation across the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced final educational packages.

The strongest distinctions were observed in:

- explanation depth;
- scaffolding;
- cognitive demand;
- exercise difficulty;
- self-assessment difficulty.

Vocabulary complexity showed a more moderate distinction because the same Grade 8 curriculum terminology was intentionally maintained across all three conditions.

The control criteria—curriculum grounding, learning-objective coverage, and scientific-core consistency—remained substantially stable across the conditions.

The observed differences therefore appear to reflect changes in pedagogical treatment and expected learner processing rather than the introduction of different scientific content.

---

# Grounding Observation

The Advanced initial generation also provided an important methodological observation.

As analytical complexity increased, the model initially introduced several relationships, hypothetical situations, and causal deductions that extended beyond the predefined curriculum boundary.

Targeted grounding revision removed most of these expansions while preserving the intended Advanced cognitive structure.

This suggests that increasing cognitive demand may also increase the need for explicit grounding constraints during level-aware generation.

This observation is preliminary and should not be interpreted as evidence of a general causal relationship beyond the current controlled experiments.

---

# Preliminary Interpretation

Compared with the weaker and more uneven distinction observed in the original Mitosis experiment, the refined outputs provide preliminary evidence that operationalizing the pedagogical profiles is associated with clearer learner-level differentiation in this controlled condition.

In particular, the refined profiles made the intended distinctions more observable through:

- amount of scaffolding;
- number of curriculum relationships processed together;
- learner independence;
- cognitive operation;
- task structure;
- degree of integration and justification.

The findings should be interpreted as preliminary proof-of-concept evidence rather than validation of the complete framework or evidence of educational effectiveness.

---

# Limitation

This analysis is researcher-based and evaluates generated educational artifacts rather than actual learner performance.

The refined Mitosis experiment represents one lesson within one selected Grade 8 science chapter.

Formal expert evaluation and learner-level performance evaluation remain outside the scope of this proof-of-concept comparison at this stage.
