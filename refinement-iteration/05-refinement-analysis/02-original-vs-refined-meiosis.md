# Original vs Refined Meiosis – Evidence-Based Comparison

## 1. Purpose

This analysis compares the original Meiosis experiment with the refined Meiosis iteration using the same ten predefined comparison criteria.

The original experiment showed partial and uneven differentiation across the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced packages. The clearest differences were observed in Cognitive Demand, Exercise Difficulty, and Self-Assessment Difficulty, while Vocabulary Complexity, Explanation Depth, and Scaffolding Level showed weaker differentiation, particularly between the Intermediate and Advanced conditions.

The Pedagogical Profiles were subsequently refined by replacing broad descriptions such as "high," "moderate," and "low" with more operational rules related to:

- vocabulary and sentence formulation;
- number of curriculum relationships processed;
- explanation depth;
- concept integration;
- scaffolding;
- learner independence;
- cognitive demand;
- exercise structure;
- self-assessment demands.

The purpose of the present comparison is to examine whether the refined iteration produced observable changes in the previously weak differentiation dimensions while preserving curriculum grounding, Learning Objective coverage, and scientific-core consistency.

This comparison does not evaluate educational effectiveness or learner performance. It evaluates differences observed in the generated educational packages.

---

## 2. Comparison Basis

The comparison uses the accepted final outputs from:

### Original Meiosis Experiment

- Original Basic Final Output
- Original Intermediate Final Output
- Original Advanced Final Output

### Refined Meiosis Iteration

- Refined Basic Final Output
- Refined Intermediate Final Output
- Refined Advanced Final Output

Initial generations and revision steps are retained as process evidence but are not used as the final level conditions in this comparison.

The same ten criteria are used for both iterations:

1. Curriculum Grounding
2. Learning Objectives Coverage
3. Vocabulary Complexity
4. Explanation Depth
5. Scaffolding Level
6. Cognitive Demand
7. Exercise Difficulty
8. Self-Assessment Difficulty
9. Scientific Core Consistency
10. Overall Level Differentiation

The comparison focuses on changes in the observable distinction among Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced outputs.

Text length alone is not considered evidence of improvement.

---

## 3. Control vs Differentiation Criteria

### Control Criteria

The following criteria should remain stable rather than become increasingly different:

- Curriculum Grounding
- Learning Objectives Coverage
- Scientific Core Consistency

Maintaining these criteria supports the interpretation that level differentiation concerns pedagogical treatment rather than different scientific content.

### Differentiation Criteria

The following criteria are expected to show progression across levels:

- Vocabulary Complexity
- Explanation Depth
- Scaffolding Level
- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

Overall Level Differentiation summarizes the pattern across these dimensions.

## 5. Detailed Analysis of the Previously Weak Dimensions

### 5.1 Vocabulary Complexity

#### Original Iteration

Vocabulary differentiation was limited.

The Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced outputs relied on substantially the same scientific terms. The Advanced condition sometimes used more academically developed wording, but this did not consistently represent a clearly different vocabulary level.

#### Refined Iteration

The same core Grade 8 terminology continued to be used, as required by the curriculum-grounding constraint.

However, the language treatment became somewhat more differentiated:

- Basic used direct and accessible definitions.
- Intermediate used the same terminology within more relational formulations.
- Advanced used the same terminology within more integrated and analytical formulations.

#### Comparison

**Original: Limited differentiation**  
→ **Refined: Moderate differentiation**

This represents an observable but limited change.

Vocabulary remains the least differentiated dimension because the design intentionally avoids introducing additional or higher-grade scientific terminology merely to make the Advanced condition appear more difficult.

**Finding: Some improvement observed, but this dimension remains only moderately differentiated.**

---

### 5.2 Explanation Depth

#### Original Iteration

The original Basic package was more direct than the higher levels.

However, the distinction between Intermediate and Advanced was less consistent. Some Advanced explanations were more developed or academically phrased without consistently demonstrating a substantially different explanatory treatment.

#### Refined Iteration

A more observable structure emerged:

**Basic**
- presents curriculum information mainly in separate, direct units;

**Intermediate**
- explicitly connects related pieces of information, such as cell quantity with chromosome count and chromosome reduction with stability;

**Advanced**
- integrates multiple pieces of fixed curriculum information within broader explanatory relationships.

For example, the Advanced treatment connects:

- reproductive-cell location;
- nuclear division;
- gamete production;
- quantitative progression;
- chromosome reduction;
- chromosome-number stability.

#### Comparison

**Original: Limited–Moderate differentiation**  
→ **Refined: Clear differentiation**

The observed change is not based only on longer explanations. It reflects an increase in the number and integration of curriculum relationships processed within the explanation.

**Finding: Improvement observed.**

---

### 5.3 Scaffolding Level

#### Original Iteration

Basic showed more guidance than the higher levels, but Intermediate and Advanced remained relatively structured.

The reduction in support from Intermediate to Advanced was therefore not consistently strong.

#### Refined Iteration

The levels show a more observable progression in learner support.

**Basic provides high support through:**
- matching;
- word banks;
- true/false items;
- ordering;
- direct identification.

**Intermediate provides task structure but requires the learner to:**
- complete comparisons;
- sequence and explain;
- connect related concepts;
- identify and correct statements.

**Advanced requires the learner to:**
- map multiple relationships;
- evaluate models;
- synthesize several curriculum concepts;
- identify multiple errors;
- construct an integrated correction.

#### Comparison

**Original: Moderate/Partial differentiation**  
→ **Refined: Clear differentiation**

The change is observable in the decreasing amount of supplied reasoning and the increasing responsibility placed on the learner.

**Finding: Improvement observed.**

## 6. Dimensions That Were Already Strong

The original Meiosis experiment already showed clearer differentiation in:

- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

The refinement did not need to create differentiation in these dimensions from the beginning. Instead, the objective was to preserve these distinctions while improving the weaker dimensions.

### Cognitive Demand

The progression remained observable:

**Basic:** identify, recall, match, complete, order  
→ **Intermediate:** compare, sequence, explain, relate, classify  
→ **Advanced:** analyze, integrate, evaluate, synthesize, justify.

### Exercise Difficulty

The progression remained:

**guided recall and recognition**  
→ **relational application**  
→ **multi-step analysis and integration**.

### Self-Assessment Difficulty

The progression remained:

**direct understanding**  
→ **relationship explanation and application**  
→ **independent integration, evaluation, and justification**.

Therefore, these dimensions are classified as **maintained strong differentiation dimensions** rather than newly improved dimensions.

---

## 7. Control Criteria After Refinement

The refinement did not produce the observed differentiation by changing the scientific knowledge available to each level.

Across both iterations:

- the same five Learning Objectives were retained;
- the same scientific core was maintained;
- curriculum grounding remained a required control;
- Advanced difficulty was not intentionally created through additional scientific content.

This supports the intended distinction between:

> **WHAT knowledge may be used** — controlled by curriculum grounding

and

> **HOW the same knowledge is pedagogically processed** — controlled by the Pedagogical Profile.

The stability of the control criteria is therefore an important part of interpreting the refinement.

## 9. Main Finding

The criterion-based comparison indicates that the refined Meiosis iteration produced a broader and more systematic pattern of observable level differentiation than the original Meiosis experiment.

The most important observed changes occurred in two dimensions that had previously been weak:

- **Explanation Depth:** Limited–Moderate → Clear
- **Scaffolding Level:** Moderate/Partial → Clear

Vocabulary Complexity also showed some change:

- **Vocabulary Complexity:** Limited → Moderate

However, vocabulary remained less distinct than the other differentiation dimensions.

The dimensions that were already clearly differentiated in the original experiment remained strong:

- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

At the same time, the control criteria remained stable:

- Curriculum Grounding
- Learning Objectives Coverage
- Scientific Core Consistency

Therefore, the observed change is not attributable simply to giving higher levels additional scientific knowledge.

Instead, the refined outputs show a more observable progression in the pedagogical processing of the same fixed curriculum knowledge.

---

## 10. Interpretation

The results suggest that replacing broad Pedagogical Profile descriptions with more operational rules was associated with clearer observable distinctions in several output dimensions, particularly Explanation Depth and Scaffolding Level.

However, this controlled comparison does not establish causality or validate the Pedagogical Profiles.

The observed changes occurred within one lesson and one refinement iteration.

The result should therefore be interpreted as:

> **preliminary evidence that the refined operational profiles are associated with clearer level differentiation in the Meiosis proof-of-concept condition.**

It should not be interpreted as evidence of educational effectiveness, learner improvement, or validation of the complete framework.

---

## 11. Remaining Limitation

The refined profiles have currently been tested only on the Meiosis lesson.

It is therefore not yet known whether the clearer differentiation observed after refinement will also occur when the same profiles are applied to another lesson.

Vocabulary Complexity also remains less strongly differentiated than the other pedagogical dimensions.

These limitations should remain explicit when interpreting the refinement results.

---

## 12. Next Step

The refined Pedagogical Profiles should now be frozen and applied without further modification to the previously tested Mitosis lesson.

This second refined experiment should preserve:

- the same refined profile definitions;
- the same operational differentiation rules;
- the same experimental-control principles;
- the same grounding restrictions;
- the same four output components;
- the same ten evaluation criteria.

The purpose is to examine whether the differentiation pattern observed in refined Meiosis can also be observed when the same profile design is applied to a second lesson.

Only after this second refined experiment should a final cross-experiment interpretation be made regarding the consistency of the refined level-aware generation approach.
