# Original vs Refined Mitosis – Comparative Analysis

## Purpose

This analysis compares the original Mitosis experiment with the refined Mitosis iteration using the same ten predefined evaluation criteria.

The purpose is to examine whether operationalizing the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced pedagogical profiles was associated with clearer level differentiation while maintaining the same curriculum source, lesson scope, learning objectives, and scientific core.

The comparison focuses on the final accepted outputs from each iteration.

---

## Comparison Basis

The same ten predefined criteria are used:

1. Curriculum Grounding
2. Learning Objectives Coverage
3. Vocabulary Complexity
4. Explanation Depth
5. Scaffolding Level
6. Cognitive Demand
7. Exercise Difficulty
8. Self-Assessment Difficulty
9. Scientific Core Consistency
10. Overall Level Differentiation

Three criteria are treated primarily as experimental controls:

- Curriculum Grounding
- Learning Objectives Coverage
- Scientific Core Consistency

The remaining criteria are used to evaluate pedagogical differentiation.

# 1. Curriculum Grounding

## Original Experiment

The original Mitosis experiment generally maintained curriculum grounding after revision. However, increasing task complexity, particularly in the Advanced condition, initially encouraged several unsupported causal or mechanistic additions that required correction.

## Refined Experiment

The refined experiment also required targeted grounding revision, particularly in the Advanced condition. The initial Advanced generation introduced unsupported organism-specific relationships, hypothetical disruptions, and causal deductions.

These were substantially removed in the revised final output while preserving the intended Advanced cognitive structure.

## Comparison

**Control maintained**

Grounding remained an active constraint in both iterations.

The refinement was not intended to improve differentiation by introducing additional scientific knowledge. Instead, all learner levels were expected to operate within the same curriculum boundary.

An important repeated observation across the two iterations is that higher cognitive demand can coincide with increased pressure on the model to infer relationships beyond the fixed knowledge boundary.

This observation remains preliminary and should not be interpreted as a general causal finding.

---

# 2. Learning Objectives Coverage

## Original Experiment

All three original conditions addressed the five fixed Mitosis learning objectives.

## Refined Experiment

All three refined conditions continued to address the same five learning objectives.

## Comparison

**Control maintained**

No meaningful change was expected or required.

The refinement changed the intended pedagogical treatment of the objectives rather than changing the objectives themselves.

---

# 3. Vocabulary Complexity

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Limited differentiation**

The original Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced packages relied heavily on the same curriculum terminology.

Some Advanced wording was more academically developed, but the distinction between Intermediate and Advanced was not consistently strong.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Moderate differentiation**

The same Grade 8 scientific terminology was intentionally retained, but the way terminology was used became more differentiated.

- Basic used terms in direct definitions and simple relationships.
- Intermediate used terminology across connected curriculum relationships.
- Advanced used the same terminology within integrated stage–structure–outcome analysis.

## Observed Change

**Limited → Moderate**

### Interpretation

Some improvement was observed.

However, vocabulary remained less differentiated than other pedagogical dimensions because the experiment intentionally preserved the same curriculum terminology across learner levels.

This should not be interpreted as a failure of refinement. Introducing unnecessary higher-grade terminology would conflict with the controlled curriculum scope.

---

# 4. Explanation Depth

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Moderate differentiation**

The original Basic condition was relatively direct, while Intermediate and Advanced contained greater elaboration.

However, some of the distinction between Intermediate and Advanced appeared to result from longer or more academically developed wording rather than consistently different conceptual processing.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

The refined profiles produced a more observable progression:

- Basic presented curriculum facts in small, sequential units.
- Intermediate explicitly connected related curriculum facts.
- Advanced organized and integrated multiple relationships across stages, structures, purposes, and outcomes.

## Observed Change

**Moderate → Clear**

### Interpretation

A meaningful improvement was observed in explanation depth.

The refined profiles provided more operational guidance concerning how many concepts should be processed together and how relationships should be represented.

---

# 5. Scaffolding Level

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Moderate / Partial differentiation**

The original outputs showed some difference in learner support, but the distinction between Intermediate and Advanced was not consistently strong.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

The refined conditions showed a more systematic progression:

### Basic
- high scaffolding;
- direct instructions;
- guided recognition and sequencing;
- low learner independence.

### Intermediate
- moderate scaffolding;
- guided comparison and classification;
- relationship identification;
- moderate learner independence.

### Advanced
- low scaffolding;
- multi-part evaluation;
- multi-stage integration;
- justification;
- greater learner independence.

## Observed Change

**Moderate / Partial → Clear**

### Interpretation

Scaffolding showed one of the clearest improvements following profile operationalization.

The refined profiles described observable task characteristics rather than relying only on broad labels such as Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced.

---

# 6. Cognitive Demand

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

The original experiment already showed a meaningful progression from recall and understanding toward application and analysis.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

The refined experiment preserved this progression while defining the cognitive expectations more explicitly:

- Basic: Remember + Understand
- Intermediate: Understand + Apply
- Advanced: Apply + Analyze

The Advanced condition additionally emphasized multi-step integration and evidence-based justification using the same fixed knowledge.

## Observed Change

**Clear → Clear**

### Interpretation

The refinement did not need to create a new distinction in this criterion because cognitive demand was already one of the strongest differentiated dimensions in the original experiment.

Instead, the refinement maintained and made this distinction more operationally explicit.

---

# 7. Exercise Difficulty

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

Exercise difficulty was already one of the stronger dimensions in the original Mitosis experiment.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

The refined exercise progression became more explicitly linked to the pedagogical profiles:

- Basic: direct and highly guided tasks.
- Intermediate: comparison, classification, correction, sequencing with explanation, and simple application.
- Advanced: multi-fact evaluation, multi-stage integration, multi-step processing, and justification.

## Observed Change

**Clear → Clear**

### Interpretation

The strong distinction was maintained.

The main refinement was not simply increasing difficulty, but making the source of difficulty more controlled and pedagogically interpretable.

---

# 8. Self-Assessment Difficulty

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Clear overall differentiation**

The original self-assessment questions showed meaningful differences in cognitive demand, although some Intermediate–Advanced overlap remained.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Clear differentiation**

The refined conditions showed a more systematic progression:

- Basic checked direct recall and understanding.
- Intermediate required relationships between two or closely related curriculum elements.
- Advanced required integration across multiple stages, structures, purposes, and outcomes.

## Observed Change

**Clear overall → Clear**

### Interpretation

The distinction remained strong and became more systematically aligned with the operational profiles.

---

# 9. Scientific Core Consistency

## Original Experiment

The same core Mitosis content was maintained across learner levels.

## Refined Experiment

The same scientific core was again maintained across the three refined conditions after grounding revision.

## Observed Change

**Control maintained**

### Interpretation

This stability is important because it indicates that learner-level differentiation was not created by assigning more scientific content to Advanced learners.

The intended difference concerned pedagogical processing of the same curriculum knowledge.

# 10. Overall Level Differentiation

## Original Experiment

**Classification: Partial and uneven**

The original experiment demonstrated some meaningful differentiation.

The clearest distinctions occurred in:

- cognitive demand;
- exercise difficulty;
- self-assessment difficulty.

However, weaker differentiation was observed in:

- vocabulary complexity;
- explanation depth;
- scaffolding.

Basic was generally more distinguishable, while Intermediate and Advanced showed greater overlap.

## Refined Experiment

**Classification: Clearer and more systematic differentiation, although not uniform across every criterion**

The refined experiment showed a more observable progression from Basic to Intermediate to Advanced.

The strongest distinctions were observed in:

- explanation depth;
- scaffolding;
- cognitive demand;
- exercise difficulty;
- self-assessment difficulty.

Vocabulary complexity remained more moderately differentiated.

## Observed Change

**Partial and uneven → Clearer and more systematic**

# Main Comparative Finding

The comparison provides preliminary evidence that operationalizing the pedagogical profiles was associated with clearer level differentiation in the Mitosis proof-of-concept condition.

The most notable changes were:

- Vocabulary Complexity: **Limited → Moderate**
- Explanation Depth: **Moderate → Clear**
- Scaffolding Level: **Moderate / Partial → Clear**

Dimensions that were already strongly differentiated in the original experiment remained strong:

- Cognitive Demand: **Clear → Clear**
- Exercise Difficulty: **Clear → Clear**
- Self-Assessment Difficulty: **Clear overall → Clear**

At the same time, the experimental controls remained substantially stable:

- Curriculum Grounding
- Learning Objectives Coverage
- Scientific Core Consistency

This pattern is important because the observed improvement was not based on providing different scientific knowledge to different learner levels.

Instead, the refinement primarily changed how the same knowledge was structured, connected, processed, and assessed.

---

# Interpretation of the Refinement

The original pedagogical profiles expressed meaningful general differences among Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced learners, but some dimensions remained too broad to consistently guide generation.

The refined profiles operationalized these differences using more observable generation requirements, including:

- number of concepts processed together;
- degree of relationship integration;
- level of scaffolding;
- learner independence;
- cognitive operation;
- task structure;
- amount of required justification.

The observed Mitosis results suggest that these operational distinctions were associated with clearer generated differences, particularly between Intermediate and Advanced.

However, because this is a controlled proof-of-concept experiment, the result should not be interpreted as proof that the refined profiles are educationally effective or universally applicable.

---

# Grounding and Differentiation Trade-Off Observation

Both the original and refined Mitosis experiments showed that increasing cognitive complexity can create pressure for the model to move beyond the predefined knowledge boundary.

In the refined Advanced initial output, this appeared through hypothetical disruptions, causal deductions, and broader organism-specific relationships.

Targeted revision improved grounding while preserving Advanced-level analytical processing.

This provides preliminary design evidence that level-aware prompting should not operate independently from grounding control.

In the proposed framework:

- the pedagogical profile should control **how the retrieved knowledge is processed and presented**;
- the grounding mechanism should continue to control **what knowledge can be used**.

This observation supports maintaining both functions as distinct but interacting components of the framework.

---

# Limitations

This comparison is based on researcher analysis of generated educational artifacts.

It does not establish:

- learner achievement;
- educational effectiveness;
- generalizability across subjects or grade levels;
- expert validation of the generated packages.

The comparison is limited to one Mitosis lesson from the selected Palestinian Grade 8 science chapter.

---

# Conclusion

The refined Mitosis iteration showed clearer and more systematic level differentiation than the original Mitosis experiment, particularly in explanation depth and scaffolding, while maintaining strong differences in cognitive demand, exercise difficulty, and self-assessment difficulty.

Vocabulary differentiation also improved but remained more moderate because the same curriculum terminology was intentionally preserved across levels.

These findings provide preliminary proof-of-concept evidence that operationalizing pedagogical profiles can improve the observability of level-aware generation within a controlled curriculum-grounded setting.

They do not constitute validation of the complete framework or evidence of educational effectiveness.
