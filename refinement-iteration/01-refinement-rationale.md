# Pedagogical Profile Refinement – Rationale

## Purpose

This refinement iteration was introduced following the criterion-based analysis of the Mitosis and Meiosis proof-of-concept experiments.

The purpose is to improve the operational distinction between the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced Pedagogical Profiles before proceeding to the broader implementation of the proposed framework.

The original experiments, prompts, outputs, and analyses remain unchanged as historical experimental records.

---

## Basis for Refinement

The cross-experiment analysis showed that level differentiation was partial and uneven across the predefined criteria.

The clearest differentiation was observed in:

- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

Weaker differentiation was observed in:

- Vocabulary Complexity
- Explanation Depth
- Scaffolding Level

The Basic packages were generally more clearly distinguishable from the higher-level packages, while the distinction between Intermediate and Advanced was less consistent.

Some differences, particularly at the Advanced level, reflected increased text length or more academically developed wording rather than a consistently distinct pedagogical treatment.

---

## Refinement Principle

The refinement does not change the scientific knowledge assigned to each learner level.

The same curriculum knowledge boundary and Learning Objectives will continue to apply across Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced conditions.

The refinement focuses on making the **HOW** dimension of level-aware generation more explicit and observable.

Instead of relying mainly on general descriptions such as:

- high, moderate, or low scaffolding;
- simple, moderate, or advanced explanation;
- low, moderate, or high learner independence;

the refined Pedagogical Profiles will define operational rules that can be directly observed in the generated outputs.

These rules will address:

- vocabulary and sentence complexity;
- explanation depth;
- concept relationships;
- concept load;
- scaffolding;
- learner independence;
- cognitive demand;
- exercise design; and
- self-assessment design.

---

## Refinement Objective

The objective is to determine whether more explicit and operational Pedagogical Profiles can produce clearer differentiation among Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced educational packages while maintaining:

- the same curriculum knowledge boundary;
- the same scientific Learning Objectives;
- curriculum grounding; and
- scientific core consistency.

The refinement is intended as an additional controlled iteration and does not replace the original experiments.

---

## Evaluation Approach

The refined outputs will be compared using the same predefined comparison criteria used in the original experiments.

Particular attention will be given to the criteria that showed weaker differentiation in the first iteration:

- Vocabulary Complexity
- Explanation Depth
- Scaffolding Level

The analysis will also examine whether the previously clearer differences in:

- Cognitive Demand
- Exercise Difficulty
- Self-Assessment Difficulty

remain observable after refinement.

Text length alone will not be treated as evidence of successful level differentiation.

---

## Research Position

This refinement represents an iterative design step based on the findings of the initial proof-of-concept experiments.

Its purpose is to improve and retest the level-aware generation component before proceeding to implementation of the broader proposed framework according to the research methodology.
