## 1. Explanations

### What is Meiosis?

- **Meiosis (الانقسام المنصف)** is a type of cell division where the nucleus of a reproductive cell divides.
- It forms **four daughter cells** called **gametes (غاميتات)**.

### Where Does Meiosis Take Place?

- Meiosis occurs only in **reproductive (sexual) cells**.
- These cells are located inside the **male and female sex organs** of most animals and plants.

### How Does the Chromosome Number Change?

- A parent reproductive cell starts with a full number of chromosomes (such as **46 chromosomes** in a human cell).
- During meiosis, the chromosome number is cut in half.
- Each resulting gamete ends up with **half the original number of chromosomes** (such as **23 chromosomes** in human gametes).

### What is the Numerical Output of Meiosis?

- **1 parent cell** (with 46 chromosomes) divides into 2 intermediate cells (with 23 chromosomes each).
- These divide again to form **4 final gametes** (each containing 23 chromosomes).

### Why is Meiosis Important?

- Meiosis helps maintain the **constancy and stability of chromosome numbers** in the cells of a living species across generations.

## 2. Key Vocabulary

- **Meiosis (الانقسام المنصف)**: A cell division process in reproductive cells that produces four gametes, each having half the original chromosome count.
- **Reproductive / Sexual Cells (الخلايا التناسلية / الجنسية)**: Specialized cells in male and female sex organs that divide by meiosis.
- **Gametes (الغاميتات)**: The four daughter cells formed by meiosis that contain half the parent cell's chromosomes.
- **Chromosome Number Reduction**: The reduction of chromosomes from the full parent number (46 in humans) to half (23 in gametes).
