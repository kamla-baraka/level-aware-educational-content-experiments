# Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge

## Experiment

Experiment 2 – Meiosis

## Purpose

Before generating the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced educational packages, curriculum knowledge relevant to the five fixed learning objectives was retrieved from the uploaded Palestinian Grade 8 Science curriculum source.

This retrieved knowledge was then fixed and used as the common knowledge boundary for all three learner levels.

## LO1 – Location of Meiosis in Living Organisms

The retrieved curriculum knowledge included:

- Meiosis occurs exclusively in reproductive / sexual cells.
- These reproductive cells are responsible for producing gametes.
- Reproductive cells are located within the male and female sex organs of most animals and plants.

## LO2 – Definition of Meiosis and Production of Four Gametes

The retrieved curriculum knowledge included:

- Meiosis is a form of cell division in which the nucleus of a parent reproductive cell divides.
- One parent reproductive cell produces four daughter cells.
- The four resulting daughter cells are called gametes.

## LO3 – Chromosome-Number Reduction During Meiosis

The retrieved curriculum knowledge established that:

- Each daughter cell (gamete) produced by meiosis contains half the original number of chromosomes found in the parent cell.
- In the human example presented in the curriculum, the parent reproductive cell contains 46 chromosomes.
- The resulting gametes contain 23 chromosomes each.

## LO4 – Quantitative Results of Meiosis

The fixed quantitative relationships included:

- 1 parent reproductive cell produces exactly 4 daughter cells (gametes).
- Each of the four daughter cells contains half the chromosome count of the parent cell.
- The curriculum diagram presents the following numerical progression for human cells:

  1. Parent cell: 46 chromosomes.
  2. Intermediate progression: 2 cells with 23 chromosomes each.
  3. Final result: 4 daughter cells (gametes) with 23 chromosomes each.

## LO5 – Biological Significance of Meiosis

The retrieved curriculum knowledge established that:

- Meiosis contributes directly to maintaining the constancy and stability of chromosome numbers in the cells of a given species of living organisms across generations.

## Knowledge Boundary

This fixed retrieved curriculum knowledge was used unchanged for the Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced generation conditions.

The learner level was not intended to change the scientific knowledge available for generation. Level differentiation was intended to change how the same knowledge was explained, connected, practiced, and assessed.

The fixed knowledge did not include:

- Down Syndrome
- Meiotic errors / non-disjunction
- Mitosis
- Meiosis I or Meiosis II
- Named meiotic stages
- Crossing over
- Homologous chromosome pairing
- Genetic variation
- Fertilization
- Other mechanisms or external scientific knowledge not explicitly included in the fixed Meiosis scope
