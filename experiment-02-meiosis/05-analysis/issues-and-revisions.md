# Issues and Revisions

## Experiment

Experiment 2 – Meiosis

## Purpose

This document records the grounding-related issues identified during researcher review of the generated Meiosis educational packages and the revisions applied before the final cross-level comparison.

The purpose of the revisions was to improve adherence to the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge while preserving the intended Pedagogical Profile for each learner level.

These observations represent researcher-based proof-of-concept analysis and should not be interpreted as formal expert evaluation.

---

## Basic Level

### Initial Review

The Basic-Level package was accepted without a grounding revision.

The generated package:

- remained within the fixed Meiosis curriculum scope;
- covered the five fixed Learning Objectives;
- followed the required four-component Output Structure;
- used simple vocabulary and high scaffolding;
- focused mainly on Remember and Understand;
- did not introduce the excluded Meiosis mechanisms or external scientific content identified during researcher review.

### Revision Status

**No revision required.**

---

## Intermediate Level

### Issues Identified in the Initial Output

The initial Intermediate package increased the cognitive demand toward Understand and Apply, but researcher review identified several elements that extended beyond the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge.

#### 1. Hypothetical Chromosome Numbers

The initial output introduced examples using:

- 14 chromosomes;
- 48 chromosomes;
- 64 chromosomes.

These numerical examples were not part of the fixed curriculum knowledge.

The fixed numerical example was:

**46 chromosomes in the parent reproductive cell → 23 chromosomes in the resulting cells / gametes.**

#### 2. Additional Causal Explanation

The initial Self-Assessment section stated that the formation of gametes with half the chromosome count:

> “prevents chromosome numbers from multiplying across generations”

This causal explanation went beyond the fixed curriculum statement.

The fixed knowledge stated only that meiosis:

**contributes to maintaining the constancy and stability of chromosome numbers in the cells of a given species across generations.**

#### 3. Terminology Used for the Numerical Progression

The initial package used the term:

**“Developmental Sequencing”**

This wording was not well aligned with the fixed knowledge, which presented a quantitative progression:

**1 parent cell with 46 chromosomes → 2 cells with 23 chromosomes each → 4 gametes with 23 chromosomes each.**

### Revision Applied

The Intermediate package was revised to:

- remove hypothetical chromosome numbers;
- use only the fixed 46 → 23 human example for numerical application;
- remove the additional causal explanation concerning chromosome-number stability;
- replace “Developmental Sequencing” with terminology based on the fixed quantitative progression;
- retain comparison, sequencing, relationships, and simple application;
- preserve the Intermediate Pedagogical Profile and its Understand-and-Apply cognitive demand.

### Revision Status

**Accepted after grounding revision.**

---

## Advanced Level

### Issues Identified in the Initial Output

The initial Advanced package increased reasoning and integration, but researcher review identified several analytical extensions beyond the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge.

#### 1. Combined Chromosome Calculation

The initial package calculated:

**4 × 23 = 92 chromosomes**

and interpreted this as the combined chromosome count across all four gametes.

Although this value can be mathematically derived from the generated data, the fixed curriculum knowledge focused on the chromosome number **per cell**, not on a combined chromosome total across separate cells.

The calculation was therefore removed to maintain the intended curriculum boundary.

#### 2. Hypothetical Failure Scenario

The initial package introduced a hypothetical scenario in which chromosome reduction did not occur and the resulting gametes retained 46 chromosomes.

This scenario was not part of the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge.

#### 3. Additional Causal Interpretation

The initial package stated that chromosome reduction prevents chromosome numbers from:

> “doubling uncontrollably”

This explanation introduced a causal interpretation beyond the fixed curriculum statement concerning chromosome-number stability.

#### 4. Additional Functional Framing

The initial package referred to:

**“two primary functions of meiosis”**

This framing was more interpretive than the fixed curriculum knowledge and was therefore removed from the revised package.

### Revision Applied

The Advanced package was revised so that higher cognitive demand was created through relationships already available in the fixed knowledge.

The revised package retained Advanced-level reasoning through:

- analysis of the fixed numerical progression;
- the **1 : 4** relationship between the initial parent cell and final gametes;
- the **23 : 46 = 1 : 2** chromosome-number relationship per cell;
- comparison between the parent reproductive cell and the resulting gametes;
- relationship mapping;
- evidence-based identification of meiosis;
- interpretation of fixed curriculum data;
- integrated synthesis using multiple pieces of fixed knowledge.

The hypothetical failure scenario, combined chromosome-total calculation, and unsupported causal explanations were removed.

### Revision Status

**Accepted after grounding revision.**

---

## Minor Wording Observations in the Final Outputs

A small number of expressions in the generated outputs used wording slightly broader than the most precise wording of the fixed knowledge.

For example, expressions referring to the “halving of genetic material” or “genetic material quantity per cell” are broader than the fixed curriculum focus on **chromosome number per cell**.

Where these expressions occurred, the surrounding explanation immediately grounded the relationship in chromosome counts such as 46 and 23.

These wording observations were recorded but did not lead to an additional revision cycle.

---

## Cross-Level Revision Pattern

Within this controlled experiment:

- The Basic package was accepted without grounding revision.
- The Intermediate package required grounding revision.
- The Advanced package required grounding revision.

The revisions improved adherence to the Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge while preserving the intended differentiation among the three Pedagogical Profiles.

This pattern is an observation from the current proof-of-concept experiment. It does **not** establish that increasing cognitive demand causes grounding problems.

Instead, it suggests that maintaining a strict curriculum boundary may require stronger grounding controls when generation tasks request greater application, analysis, integration, or justification.

---

## Experimental Interpretation

The revision process highlighted an important distinction in the proposed framework:

- **Curriculum grounding controls what knowledge may be used.**
- **The Pedagogical Profile controls how that knowledge is presented, practiced, and assessed.**

The Meiosis experiment showed that increasing level difficulty does not require introducing additional scientific knowledge.

Intermediate and Advanced differentiation could still be maintained after removing unsupported content by changing the depth of relationships, cognitive demand, scaffolding, comparison, interpretation, and integration applied to the same fixed curriculum knowledge.

---

## Scope of These Findings

These observations are limited to a controlled proof-of-concept experiment using one selected Meiosis section from the Palestinian Grade 8 Science curriculum.

They should not be interpreted as:

- validation of the complete proposed framework;
- evidence of learner effectiveness;
- formal expert evaluation;
- proof of a causal relationship between cognitive demand and grounding difficulty;
- validation of a complete technical RAG pipeline; or
- evidence of generalizability across other lessons, subjects, curricula, or LLM environments.
