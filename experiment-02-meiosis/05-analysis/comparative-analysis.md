# Comparative Analysis

## Experiment

Experiment 2 – Meiosis

## Purpose

This document compares the final Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced Meiosis educational packages after the grounding revisions were completed.

The comparison examines whether meaningful level differentiation was maintained while the curriculum source, Meiosis scope, five Learning Objectives, Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge, and four-component Output Structure remained fixed.

This is a researcher-based proof-of-concept comparison and does not represent formal expert evaluation.

---

## Comparison Criteria

The final outputs were compared using the same ten criteria applied in Experiment 1 – Mitosis:

1. Curriculum Grounding
2. Learning Objectives Coverage
3. Vocabulary Complexity
4. Explanation Depth
5. Scaffolding Level
6. Cognitive Demand
7. Exercise Difficulty
8. Self-Assessment Difficulty
9. Scientific Core Consistency
10. Overall Level Differentiation

## 1. Curriculum Grounding

The final Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced packages were all based on the same Fixed Retrieved Curriculum Knowledge.

The Basic package was accepted without a grounding revision.

The initial Intermediate and Advanced packages contained elements that extended beyond the fixed knowledge boundary. These elements were removed or revised before the final comparison.

The final comparison therefore concerns the accepted final versions rather than the uncorrected initial generations.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives Coverage

All three final packages addressed the same five fixed Learning Objectives.

The learner level did not change the scientific learning goals.

Instead, the difference among levels concerned how the learner was expected to engage with the same objectives.

For example:

- Basic emphasized identifying and recalling the main relationships.
- Intermediate required sequencing, comparison, and simple application.
- Advanced required interpretation, relationship analysis, justification, and synthesis.

---

## 3. Vocabulary Complexity

The Basic package used simpler wording and provided direct definitions of the essential terms.

The Intermediate package used curriculum scientific terminology more consistently and connected terms such as reproductive cells, gametes, chromosome-number reduction, and chromosome stability.

The Advanced package retained the same scientific terminology but used it within more integrated explanations and analytical relationships.

Therefore, vocabulary complexity increased without requiring the introduction of additional scientific concepts outside the fixed curriculum boundary.

---

## 4. Explanation Depth

The Basic explanations presented the main Meiosis concepts separately and directly.

The Intermediate explanations connected several fixed concepts, particularly:

- location of meiosis;
- reproductive cells;
- production of four gametes;
- chromosome-number reduction; and
- the fixed quantitative progression.

The Advanced explanations integrated these concepts more deeply and analyzed numerical relationships such as:

- **1 parent cell : 4 final gametes**
- **23 : 46 = 1 : 2** for chromosome number per cell.

These relationships were derived directly from the fixed curriculum data rather than from additional biological knowledge.

---

## 5. Scaffolding Level

A clear difference in instructional support was observed across the final packages.

### Basic

The Basic package provided high scaffolding through:

- short explanations;
- direct definitions;
- simple examples;
- fill-in-the-blank activities;
- True/False questions; and
- explicit answer guides.

### Intermediate

The Intermediate package reduced support and required the learner to:

- complete quantitative information;
- arrange the fixed progression;
- compare the parent reproductive cell and resulting gametes; and
- explain relationships using the curriculum data.

### Advanced

The Advanced package used lower scaffolding and required greater learner independence through:

- analysis of numerical relationships;
- analytical comparison;
- relationship mapping;
- evidence-based identification; and
- integrated synthesis.

---

## 6. Cognitive Demand

The final outputs reflected the intended cognitive progression defined in the Pedagogical Profiles.

### Basic

**Remember and Understand**

The learner mainly recalls definitions, identifies locations and outputs, and demonstrates simple understanding of chromosome-number reduction.

### Intermediate

**Understand and Apply**

The learner applies the fixed 46 → 23 relationship, sequences the quantitative progression, and compares related curriculum concepts.

### Advanced

**Apply and Analyze**

The learner analyzes relationships within the fixed numerical data, interprets evidence, compares multiple characteristics, and synthesizes several fixed concepts into an integrated explanation.

The Advanced package was not made more difficult by adding higher-grade biological content.

---

## 7. Exercise Difficulty

Exercise difficulty increased across the three final packages while remaining based on the same curriculum knowledge.

The Basic exercises emphasized recall and recognition.

The Intermediate exercises required simple application, sequencing, and comparison.

The Advanced exercises required analysis of relationships, interpretation of fixed numerical data, and construction of a relationship map.

This progression demonstrates that exercise difficulty can be varied through the type of cognitive operation requested rather than through expansion of the scientific knowledge base.

---

## 8. Self-Assessment Difficulty

The Basic Self-Assessment Questions focused on:

- location of meiosis;
- chromosome count in the fixed human example; and
- the stated biological significance.

The Intermediate Self-Assessment Questions required:

- explanation of relationships;
- description of the quantitative progression;
- comparison of chromosome numbers; and
- identification of biological significance.

The Advanced Self-Assessment Questions required:

- identification of meiosis using multiple pieces of evidence;
- interpretation of the complete numerical progression; and
- synthesis of location, cell type, cell output, chromosome-number reduction, and biological significance.

---

## 9. Scientific Core Consistency

After grounding revision, the same scientific core was maintained across the three final packages:

- Meiosis occurs in reproductive / sexual cells.
- These cells are located in the male and female sex organs of most animals and plants.
- One parent reproductive cell produces four daughter cells called gametes.
- Each gamete contains half the chromosome number of the parent reproductive cell.
- The fixed human example uses 46 chromosomes in the parent cell and 23 chromosomes in the resulting cells / gametes.
- The curriculum presents the quantitative progression of 1 parent cell → 2 cells → 4 gametes.
- Meiosis contributes to maintaining the constancy and stability of chromosome numbers across generations.

The level differentiation therefore did not require changing the core scientific content.

---

## 10. Overall Level Differentiation

The final outputs showed meaningful differences among the three learner levels.

### Basic

The Basic package emphasized:

- simple language;
- high scaffolding;
- essential concepts;
- direct recall; and
- simple understanding.

### Intermediate

The Intermediate package emphasized:

- curriculum terminology;
- moderate scaffolding;
- relationships among concepts;
- sequencing;
- comparison; and
- simple application.

### Advanced

The Advanced package emphasized:

- integrated use of curriculum terminology;
- low scaffolding;
- greater learner independence;
- interpretation;
- analytical comparison;
- evidence-based justification; and
- synthesis.

The observed differentiation was therefore primarily pedagogical and cognitive rather than based on introducing different scientific knowledge for each learner level.

---

## Researcher Interpretation

The final Meiosis outputs provide preliminary evidence that the same curriculum-grounded knowledge can be presented in meaningfully differentiated forms by changing the Pedagogical Profile while keeping the main content-related variables fixed.

In this experiment:

**Basic → High Scaffolding → Remember / Understand**

**Intermediate → Moderate Scaffolding → Understand / Apply**

**Advanced → Low Scaffolding → Apply / Analyze**

The experiment also showed that the intended differentiation could be preserved after grounding-related content in the Intermediate and Advanced initial generations was removed.

This supports the design distinction used in the proposed framework:

- **Curriculum grounding controls WHAT knowledge is available for generation.**
- **The Pedagogical Profile controls HOW that knowledge is explained, structured, practiced, and assessed.**

---

## Preliminary Conclusion

Experiment 2 provides preliminary proof-of-concept evidence that a fixed Meiosis curriculum knowledge base can support Basic, Intermediate, and Advanced educational packages without intentionally changing the underlying scientific core.

The final outputs differed in vocabulary complexity, explanation depth, scaffolding, cognitive demand, exercise difficulty, self-assessment difficulty, and learner independence.

At the same time, the revision process showed that maintaining the fixed curriculum boundary required additional grounding control in the initial Intermediate and Advanced generations.

These findings represent preliminary feasibility observations from a controlled experiment. They should not be interpreted as validation of the complete proposed framework, evidence of learner effectiveness, formal expert evaluation, or evidence of generalizability beyond the current experimental context.
